New EU Traceability Rules Push Thermal Strip Producers Toward Fiber Laser Marking

As new European construction product traceability regulations take effect this year, manufacturers of PA66 GF25 thermal break strips are increasingly adopting automatic fiber laser marking systems to ensure compliance and protect product integrity. According to technical experts at KINGVAN TECHNOLOGY CO., LTD., the shift marks a decisive moment for the aluminum window supply chain, where permanent marking has become a prerequisite for market access.

The updated regulatory framework requires construction component manufacturers to maintain clear, durable identification of materials, batches, and production origins throughout the supply chain. For thermal break strips — the polyamide profiles that bridge the inner and outer aluminum frames of energy-efficient windows — the requirement poses a significant challenge. Conventional marking methods such as inkjet printing or embossing can fade, rub off, or even create stress points in the profile, leading to quality disputes during installation and inspection.

Fiber laser marking offers a solution that is both permanent and non-contact. The laser beam creates a high-contrast, oxidation-resistant mark directly on the surface of the strip without altering its mechanical properties. This is particularly important for PA66 GF25 material, which is reinforced with 25% glass fiber and must maintain precise dimensional tolerance for optimal thermal performance.

“We are seeing a clear shift from batch-level identification to piece-level traceability,” said a senior applications engineer at KINGVAN TECHNOLOGY. “European window fabricators and building inspectors now expect every thermal break strip to carry a readable, permanent mark that can be traced back to the raw material batch and production line. This is driving our customers to invest in automatic inline laser marking as an integral part of their extrusion processes.”

KINGVAN TECHNOLOGY, founded in Beijing, has specialized exclusively in automatic fiber laser marking equipment for thermal insulation strips since its inception. The company offers four core configurations designed to match different production line layouts and speed requirements: Single Side, Single Traction; Single Side, Double Traction; Double Side, Single Traction; and Double Side, Double Traction. Each configuration integrates an IPG fiber laser source, CE-certified components, and proprietary control software to deliver consistent marking quality at production speed.

The choice of configuration depends on the extrusion line capacity and the information density required. A single-side system is sufficient for manufacturers who mark one surface with logo, batch code, and date. Double-side systems, which allow simultaneous marking on both faces of the strip, are increasingly preferred by producers who supply multiple markets, where bilingual or dual-standard marking is mandated. Double traction modules enable higher line speeds without compromising mark accuracy, a feature that has become more critical as European and Middle Eastern markets demand faster delivery schedules.

CE certification is another critical factor in the adoption of laser marking systems. For Asian thermal strip producers exporting to Europe, equipment compliance directly affects their ability to achieve certification for their own products. KINGVAN’s machines are fully CE certified, meaning that the laser safety systems, electrical enclosures, and electromagnetic compatibility meet EU requirements. This reduces the certification burden on the end user and simplifies the integration of KINGVAN equipment into existing European extrusion lines.

The move toward laser marking also aligns with broader sustainability goals in the construction industry. Inkjet marking requires consumables such as solvents and inks, which must be managed and disposed of carefully. Laser marking eliminates these materials entirely, reducing hazardous waste and improving worker safety. Moreover, because laser marking is a dry process, there is no drying time or cooling cycle, which lowers energy consumption compared to some thermal printing systems.

Several markets beyond Europe are beginning to mirror these traceability requirements. In the Middle East, where high-performance aluminum windows are in high demand due to extreme climate conditions, project specifiers are increasingly referencing ISO and EN standards that require identification of structural components. Southeast Asian manufacturers, many of whom export to Europe, are also proactively upgrading their marking capabilities to maintain competitiveness.

KINGVAN’s technical team notes that the shift is not just about compliance; it is also about production efficiency. Modern fiber laser systems can mark a thermal break strip at linear speeds that match or exceed the output of high-capacity extrusion lines. With the Double Side, Double Traction model, producers can achieve full barcode marking on both surfaces without slowing the manufacturing process. This allows them to maintain throughput while gaining the benefit of high-resolution, machine-readable marks that improve inventory tracking and reduce errors in installation.

Looking ahead, KINGVAN expects the trend to accelerate as more regions adopt digital building permit systems that require verified product data. The company is preparing to demonstrate its latest marking solutions at several international building and window trade fairs scheduled for late 2026, where potential buyers can see live marking trials using actual PA66 GF25 profiles.

For thermal strip producers, the decision to install automatic fiber laser marking is no longer a discretionary value-add. It is fast becoming a baseline requirement for global market participation. As KINGVAN’s engineer puts it: “In the window industry, a permanent mark is permanent trust. Manufacturers who embrace this today will be better positioned to meet tomorrow’s evolving standards.”
