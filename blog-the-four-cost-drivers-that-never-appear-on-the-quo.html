The Four Cost Drivers That Never Appear on the Quote

📅 October 08, 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read

Inkjet coders, hot-stamp foil units and pre-printed labels have been marking PA66 GF25 thermal break strips for decades. They are cheap to buy, which is exactly why they survive on so many production lines. The problem is that purchase price is the smallest part of what they cost you. For a line producing roughly two million meters of thermal strip per year, consumables, downtime and rework attached to a traditional coder routinely exceed the entire cost of a fiber laser marking system within the first 18 months.

This is not an argument about which technology produces a nicer mark. It is an accounting exercise. Below is a framework you can run against your own numbers in about ten minutes.

The Four Cost Drivers That Never Appear on the Quote

When engineers compare marking options, they usually compare capital cost. That comparison is misleading because four cost drivers dominate over a five-year horizon:

  • Consumables per meter. Inkjet requires ink and solvent; hot stamping requires foil. Both scale linearly with output. Laser marking requires neither — the only recurring consumable is electricity.
  • Unplanned downtime. Printheads clog, nozzles drift, foil breaks. A single 45-minute stoppage on a strip extrusion line costs far more than a year of laser electricity.
  • Rework and scrap. Ink and foil marks sit on the surface. They smear during handling, coil winding and transport, and they fade under heat. Strips that fail a legibility check are rejected after the value has already been added.
  • Labour and compliance overhead. Solvent handling, VOC disposal, consumable ordering and printhead cleaning are all recurring labour that a laser system does not generate.

A Worked Five-Year Model

The figures below are illustrative — substitute your own throughput, power tariff and labour rate. Assume a line running two million meters of PA66 GF25 per year, a marking code of eight to twelve characters, and 6,000 operating hours annually.

Cost element Inkjet / hot stamp Fiber laser marking
Capital (mid-range system) $18,000 – $25,000 $40,000 – $60,000
Consumables per meter $0.006 – $0.012 ~$0.0004 (electricity only)
Consumables, 5 years (10M m) $60,000 – $120,000 ~$4,000
Scheduled maintenance, 5 years $12,000 – $18,000 $3,000 – $5,000
Estimated 5-year total $90,000 – $163,000 $47,000 – $69,000

Even in the most favourable traditional-method scenario, the gap is roughly $40,000 over five years — and that gap closes before you assign any value to downtime, scrap or solvent disposal. On a realistic basis, payback lands between 12 and 20 months.

Where Inkjet Still Makes Sense

Honest analysis requires acknowledging the counter-case. Traditional marking remains rational when:

  • Annual output is below roughly 150,000 meters, where consumable volume is small enough that the capital gap never closes.
  • You need multi-colour or high-resolution graphics that a laser cannot produce.
  • You mark a substrate the laser damages, or you run a multi-material line where one coding head must handle plastics, metals and coated surfaces interchangeably.
  • Your process is still at pilot scale and the specification may change within twelve months.

For a dedicated PA66 GF25 thermal strip line running at commercial volume, none of these apply.

The Rework Cost Nobody Measures

On glass-fibre reinforced polyamide, ink has a specific weakness: the fibre network at the surface creates capillary paths, and low-viscosity inks can wick along them, producing ragged or feathered edges. Foil transfer depends on a heat-and-pressure dwell that varies with line speed and strip surface temperature. Both failure modes produce marks that pass inspection on the day of production and fail three weeks later after coiling, storage and handling.

Compare the cost of a rejected aluminium window profile — fabricated with a thermal barrier that cannot be traced — against the delta in system capital. A single field failure typically erases the entire price difference between the two approaches.

Throughput and Integration

The economics improve further when marking is integrated rather than bolted on. A laser head mounted inline, triggered by an encoder signal, marks at line speed without contacting the strip. There is no dwell time, no pressure roller and no acceleration ramp, which means the marking station does not set the line's maximum velocity. Traditional coders frequently do.

This also removes a common hidden cost: the operator who walks to the coder every shift to verify print quality and clear a nozzle. With laser marking, the routine reduces to a periodic lens check and a focal-distance verification.

Capital range varies with configuration. Single-side marking costs less than double-side, and a single-traction transport is less expensive than a double-traction system that handles the strip under higher tension and at higher speed. What EN 14024 Changes About the Math

EN 14024 sets the requirements and test methods for metal profiles with thermal barriers. Profiles placed on the European market under it carry performance declarations that must be traceable back to a production batch. A marking code that is illegible at the point of audit is not a cosmetic defect — it is a documentation failure that can force batch-level hold-and-review.

When you assign even a modest probability to that event, the permanence of a laser mark stops being a quality preference and becomes a risk-control line item. Laser marking on PA66 GF25 produces a mark that survives coiling, transport, powder coating, crimping and years of service without a protective overcoat. That is the property you are actually buying.

Run the Calculation Yourself

Three inputs determine your answer:

  • Annual meters marked. Multiply by your current consumable cost per meter.
  • Hours lost per year to coder-related stoppages. Multiply by your fully absorbed line rate per hour.
  • Rejection rate attributable to illegible or missing marks. Multiply by the value at the point of rejection, not the cost of the raw strip.

If the sum of those three figures exceeds roughly one-third of the laser system's capital cost, the payback period will be under two years. In our experience with aluminium window manufacturers, it almost always does.
